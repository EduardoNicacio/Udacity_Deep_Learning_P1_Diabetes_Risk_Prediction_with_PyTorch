# Project Overview

## Project Introduction

In this project, You will build a neural network-based diabetes risk prediction model using real CDC health survey data. You will work with a dataset containing 21 diverse health and lifestyle features (including age, BMI, blood pressure, smoking status, physical activity, and medical history) to predict diabetes risk with 95-99% accuracy. The project challenges you to apply fundamental deep learning concepts to a meaningful healthcare application. You will design a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) architecture, implement proper data preprocessing for mixed data types, and select appropriate evaluation metrics that prioritize clinical relevance over raw accuracy. By the end of the project, you will produce a trained PyTorch model with a comprehensive evaluation report, demonstrating their ability to build, train, diagnose, and optimize neural networks for real-world classification tasks. The deliverable will be portfolio-ready, showcasing both technical proficiency and understanding of AI ethics in healthcare applications.

**Real-world Context**: Early detection of diabetes can reduce healthcare costs and improve patient outcomes. This model simulates a pre-screening tool that could help healthcare providers prioritize high-risk patients for diagnostic testing.

## Your Mission

**Company**: UdaciHealth
**Client**: Regional hospital network
**Problem**: Limited resources for comprehensive diabetes testing across thousands of monthly patients
**Solution**: Automated pre-screening system to prioritize diagnostic testing

**Success criteria**:
Build a classifier that catches most diabetic patients (high recall) while minimizing false alarms (acceptable precision), enabling efficient resource allocation for follow-up testing.

**Key stakeholders**:

- Clinical team (needs interpretable, actionable results)
- Hospital administrators (cost-effectiveness concerns)
- IT department (requires deployment-ready model)

### Dataset

**Source**: [CDC Diabetes Health Indicators Dataset](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/alexteboul/diabetes-health-indicators-dataset)
**Size**: ~50,000 survey responses with 50% diabetic and 50% non-diabetic entries (balanced subset from original 253K)
**Features**: 21 health and lifestyle indicators including:

- Demographics (age, sex)
- Physical measurements (BMI, blood pressure)
- Lifestyle factors (smoking, physical activity)
- Medical history (high cholesterol, stroke, heart disease)

**About the balanced dataset**: The original CDC data has ~14% diabetes prevalence. We've balanced this to 50-50 by downsampling the majority class - a valid production strategy when you have sufficient minority samples. This lets you focus on neural network fundamentals first, with more detail on this decision in the notebook.
